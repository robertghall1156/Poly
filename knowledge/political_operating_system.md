# Political Operating System

This is a living document. It is the human-readable form of the principles stored in Poly.
Edit it here and run `poly import-principles`, or edit in the Principles page and run
`poly export-principles` — both directions are supported. Poly keeps version history of every
position change.

Format: each `##` heading is a category. Each `###` heading is a principle. The bullet lines
`status` (provisional | established | retired) and `confidence` (0–1) are optional.
`Position:` is the current position; `Rationale:` explains why.

---

## Government

### Government should represent the people and provide a fair playing field
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.8

Position: Government exists to represent the people and to maintain a fair playing field. Wealth, status, or political power should not create a superior class of citizenship. Public officials should operate ethically and in service to citizens.

Rationale: Legitimacy comes from equal standing before the state. When the rules bend toward the powerful, trust in every other institution erodes with it.

### Majority rule with constitutional limits
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.8

Position: Majority rule matters, but constitutional rights and minority protections must remain intact.

Rationale: A majority that can remove the rights of a minority is not a stable democracy; it is a temporary coalition waiting to be someone else's victim.

### Power needs counterweights
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.85

Position: Power should have counterweights. No institution should accumulate unchecked power because another institution refuses to do its job.

Rationale: Most modern power grabs are enabled by abdication, not conquest. The fix is to make the abdicating institution do its job, not to bless the accumulation.

### Congress should reclaim lawmaking from the executive
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.75

Position: Congress should reclaim more lawmaking responsibility from the executive branch. The executive should execute laws rather than continuously stretching decades-old statutes into new powers.

Rationale: Delegation plus congressional gridlock has produced government by emergency declaration and reinterpretation. That is unstable, swings wildly between administrations, and escapes accountability.

### Courts should interpret, not campaign
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.7

Position: Courts should interpret and apply law rather than function as partisan institutions.

Rationale: Courts only work as a counterweight if both sides believe they are getting law rather than politics in robes.

### Hybrid representation
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.55

Position: Representation should likely use a hybrid structure combining geographic/local accountability with more proportional representation.

Rationale: Pure geographic districts reward gerrymandering and two-party lock-in; pure proportional systems lose local accountability. Mixed systems used elsewhere suggest a middle path.

### Institutions should adapt to modern society
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.6

Position: Political institutions should adapt better to modern society rather than becoming dominated permanently by the same generation or political class.

Rationale: Incumbency, seniority systems, and lifetime tenure concentrate power in a narrow cohort and slow adaptation to technological and demographic change.

## Money and political power

### Economic power should not automatically become political power
- status: established
- confidence: 0.85

Position: Economic success should be allowed and rewarded, but economic power should not automatically become political power. Money should not allow someone's political voice to become vastly louder than everyone else's.

Rationale: Political equality is the premise of representative government. Purchasing influence converts a market outcome into a governance outcome.

### Meaningful limits and transparency on political money
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.75

Position: Campaign contributions and political spending should have meaningful limits and full transparency. Political equality matters more than the ability to purchase political influence.

Rationale: Disclosure alone does not restore equality; limits alone invite evasion. Both are needed.

## Capitalism and wealth

### Capitalism with fair rules and a social floor
- status: established
- confidence: 0.85

Position: Capitalism can create enormous value and should continue to reward entrepreneurship, investment, innovation, and risk. The objective is not equal economic outcomes. The objective is a meaningful social floor, fair rules, functioning competitive markets, and preventing wealth from becoming political domination.

Rationale: Markets are the best engine of prosperity we have found; they fail when competition dies or when the winners rewrite the rules.

### Founders deserve a lot; giant companies are collective achievements
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.7

Position: Founders should benefit significantly from building companies and creating jobs. However, once a public company becomes enormous, its value is no longer produced by one individual. Employees, consumers, investors, infrastructure, public institutions, laws, courts, education, markets, and society all contribute.

Rationale: Attribution of value in a very large enterprise is genuinely shared. Policy should reflect that without punishing the act of building.

### Fix the rules before confiscating the wealth
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.7

Position: Extreme wealth concentration should be addressed mainly by repairing the rules and tax system rather than simply confiscating accumulated wealth.

Rationale: Confiscation is a one-time event that invites capital flight and legal warfare; repaired rules change the trajectory permanently.

## Social floor

### A guaranteed social floor
- status: established
- confidence: 0.85

Position: People can become extraordinarily wealthy above a social floor. Society should ensure access to essential needs including healthcare, adequate food, education, workforce training, basic housing security, and retirement security.

Rationale: A floor is what makes risk-taking, mobility, and social stability possible. It is not a ceiling.

### Phased, evidence-driven reform
- status: established
- confidence: 0.85

Position: Reform should usually be phased rather than imposed overnight. Be ambitious about the destination and pragmatic about the transition. Let people experience reforms, determine whether they work, improve them, and expand them.

Rationale: Large overnight changes create losers who mobilise against the whole project; phased reforms build constituencies and produce evidence.

## Healthcare

### Basic healthcare as a societal guarantee
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.75

Position: Basic healthcare should ultimately be treated as a societal guarantee. No one should become financially ruined because they become sick. Healthcare should not depend on employment.

Rationale: Illness is largely random with respect to merit; tying coverage to employment traps workers and punishes entrepreneurship.

### Broad public risk pooling, phased transition
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.7

Position: Healthcare financing should be broadly pooled through taxes. Healthy people already subsidize sick people through insurance; universal public risk pooling applies the same principle. The transition away from the insurance-heavy system should be phased, and people whose jobs are disrupted should receive transition and retraining support.

Rationale: The insurance principle is already accepted; the question is the size and fairness of the pool and the administrative overhead.

## Education

### Comparable public education regardless of ZIP code
- status: established
- confidence: 0.85

Position: Children should receive comparable quality public education regardless of ZIP code or local property wealth. Funding should be less dependent on local wealth.

Rationale: Property-tax funding hard-wires inequality of opportunity into geography.

### Multiple routes and second chances
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.75

Position: Four-year college should not simply be an automatic entitlement. People should demonstrate readiness or ability through multiple possible routes, and there should be second chances later in life — someone who was not academically focused at 18 should still be able to demonstrate ability at 25, 35, or later.

Rationale: Talent develops at different ages; a single high-stakes gate at 18 wastes it.

### Support the full training ecosystem
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.75

Position: Education policy should support universities, trade schools, apprenticeships, certifications, retraining, and workforce development. Corporations could participate in national mentorship/apprenticeship programs where they receive incentives in exchange for genuinely training people and creating transferable skills.

Rationale: Employers know what skills are needed; incentives should reward transferable training, not captive labour.

## Workers and corporations

### Employees should share in corporate success
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.8

Position: Companies should succeed and executives can be rewarded, but employees should participate meaningfully in corporate success. Compensation systems should encourage bonuses, profit sharing, employee equity, ownership, and broad-based incentive systems rather than relying only on permanently increasing fixed base salaries.

Rationale: Broad-based ownership aligns incentives and spreads the upside of productivity. AI and automation make this more urgent: if productivity rises while employment and household income collapse, companies eventually destroy their own consumer base.

## Executive compensation

### Tie executive pay to genuine long-term value
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.8

Position: Executive compensation should be tied to genuine long-term value creation. Extremely large disparities between executive and employee compensation can become problematic. Executives often benefit from broad market movements not attributable to their own performance. Short CEO tenure and short vesting encourage short-term decisions, and retention equity can become circular (underwater → grant more; vested → grant more). Systems should better align executives with long-term shareholders, employees, and durable business performance.

Rationale: Pay design is an incentive system; when it rewards market beta and short tenure it produces exactly that.

## Unions

### Unions as an accountable counterweight
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.75

Position: Unions are an important counterweight to concentrated corporate power, but unions must remain accountable to their members. Union leadership should not become a separate political power structure detached from workers. Support collective bargaining and worker organizing alongside transparency, democratic member control, financial accountability, and disclosure of political expenditures.

Rationale: Power needs a counterweight, and the counterweight also needs accountability.

## AI and automation

### AI should broaden prosperity
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.7

Position: AI should improve prosperity rather than simply concentrate it. If companies replace human labor at scale, society must address lost income, lost healthcare, retraining, lost purpose, consumer demand, and social stability. If AI eliminates enough jobs, some version of guaranteed income or UBI may become economically necessary — but income is only part of the problem. Work also provides purpose, structure, identity, community, and status. Future policy should combine income security with meaningful participation pathways.

Rationale: The historical pattern of automation creating new jobs may not hold at the speed and breadth of AI; planning for both outcomes is prudent.

## Taxation

### Ability to pay, with work favored over passive wealth
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.75

Position: Taxation should generally reflect ability to pay. Work should receive somewhat more favorable tax treatment than investment-derived income. Investment should still be encouraged because it involves risk, but investment income should not be so preferred that earning through wealth is structurally advantaged over earning through labor.

Rationale: The current system taxes wages more heavily than capital in practice; that inverts the incentive most people would choose.

### Extreme wealth should not escape taxation indefinitely
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.65

Position: At extreme wealth levels, unrealized gains should not remain permanently outside taxation. Extremely wealthy individuals should not be able to indefinitely substitute loans against appreciated equity for taxable income.

Rationale: "Buy, borrow, die" converts a deferral into a permanent exemption.

### Close corporate loopholes before raising rates
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.75

Position: Corporate tax reform should first focus on closing loopholes, limiting artificial avoidance, simplifying the system, enforcement, and reducing special carve-outs. Evaluate whether rates need to increase only after repairing avoidance.

Rationale: Headline rates matter less than the effective rate, which is driven by avoidance.

### Meaningful but not confiscatory inheritance taxation
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.7

Position: Very large inheritances should face meaningful taxation, but families should still be able to pass down substantial legally earned wealth. Avoid confiscatory inheritance taxation; close loopholes that allow enormous estates to effectively avoid taxes altogether.

Rationale: Dynastic wealth undermines political equality; ordinary family inheritance does not.

## Defense and foreign policy

### Strong, restrained, and efficient defense
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.8

Position: The U.S. military should defend the country, deter aggression, protect allies, honor treaties, support international stability, and promote peace whenever possible. America should remain militarily strong. Large ground interventions should generally be a last resort; supporting allies with equipment, intelligence, and weapons can sometimes prevent a larger conflict.

Rationale: Deterrence is cheaper than war; alliances multiply strength.

### No acquisition of territory for convenience
- status: established
- confidence: 0.85

Position: The United States should not acquire territory simply because it is strategically useful. Cooperate with sovereign countries rather than annex them.

Rationale: Sovereignty norms protect the U.S. too; abandoning them invites others to do the same.

### Defense spending is too high and procurement needs scrutiny
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.7

Position: Defense spending is too high overall. Pentagon procurement, contracting, contractor incentives, administrative structures, and failed programs should be aggressively reviewed for efficiency. Maintaining military strength does not require treating every defense dollar as untouchable.

Rationale: Strength and waste are not the same thing; unaudited budgets attract both.

## Immigration

### Faster, simpler legal immigration
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.8

Position: Legal immigration should be faster, simpler, and more rational. Making legal immigration realistically achievable should reduce incentives for illegal immigration. Borders and legal immigration reform should be treated together rather than as opposing positions.

Rationale: A system that takes decades to navigate legally pushes people toward the illegal route.

### Pathway for contributing residents
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.75

Position: People already living in the United States who contribute to society, work, build families, and avoid serious criminal activity should have an achievable pathway toward legal status and citizenship.

Rationale: Permanent legal limbo for millions of residents is bad for them, for the rule of law, and for the economy.

### Immigration is economically important
- status: provisional
- confidence: 0.8

Position: Immigration is economically important because of aging populations, slower population growth, workforce needs, entrepreneurship, the tax base, and future economic growth.

Rationale: Demographics are destiny for entitlement programs and growth; immigration is the main lever available.
